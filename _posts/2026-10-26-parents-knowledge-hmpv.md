Acute respiratory infections are among the leading causes of illness and death in young children worldwide. Human metapneumovirus (hMPV), first identified in 2001, can cause anything from a mild cold to bronchiolitis and pneumonia, and it drew renewed public attention in early 2025. Yet little was known about how parents in Bangladesh understand the virus or manage their children's respiratory illnesses.

## What we did

Between December 2024 and January 2025, our team interviewed 405 parents of children aged 0–12 years in Dhaka using a structured questionnaire, and held focus group discussions with 40 stakeholders, including healthcare professionals and parents. The study was published in *Microbes and Infectious Diseases* in 2025.

## What we found

- 45.4% of parents had heard of hMPV, but knowledge of its symptoms, transmission and prevention was limited.
- Respiratory infections peaked in winter, and the most common symptoms were cough, fever and wheezing.
- About a quarter of the illnesses (26%) needed hospital care.
- 81.9% of parents had given their child medicine on their own before seeking professional care.
- Limited access to healthcare, overcrowded housing and low awareness were the main challenges that parents and health workers described.

## Why it matters

Self-medication can delay diagnosis, and when it involves antibiotics, which do not work against viruses such as hMPV, it also adds to the problem of antimicrobial resistance. Participants called for targeted awareness campaigns and better paediatric healthcare services. Our findings suggest that clear public information, earlier diagnosis and community-based prevention could reduce the burden of childhood respiratory infections in Bangladesh.

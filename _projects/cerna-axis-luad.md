## Significance

The human genome encodes thousands of long non-coding RNAs, and many of them are abnormally expressed in lung adenocarcinoma. One way a lncRNA can influence tumour behaviour is by acting as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA): by binding a microRNA, it can prevent that microRNA from repressing its target messenger RNAs. Computational studies have proposed many such lncRNA–miRNA–mRNA axes in lung cancer, but far fewer have been tested in the laboratory. Validating an axis experimentally turns a statistical association into a testable mechanism, and can point to biomarkers or therapeutic targets that are worth following up.

## Project summary

For my MSc thesis, I used bioinformatic analysis of public lung adenocarcinoma datasets, including ceRNA network analysis, to prioritise one candidate lncRNA–miRNA–mRNA axis. I then designed the laboratory validation: knocking down the candidate lncRNA in lung adenocarcinoma cells, measuring the resulting expression changes along the axis by qRT-PCR, and measuring cell proliferation with the CCK-8 assay. The bioinformatics component is complete and the manuscript is in preparation. To protect unpublished work, the specific molecules and the results will be added here after publication.

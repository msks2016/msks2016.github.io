Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death worldwide, and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for about 85% of cases. Targeted therapies and immune checkpoint inhibitors have improved outcomes for some patients, but most are diagnosed at an advanced stage, and resistance to treatment is common. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have become a major focus in NSCLC research. One of the best-known lncRNAs, MALAT1, was first identified through its link to metastasis in early-stage NSCLC. Since then, hundreds of lncRNAs have been reported to influence tumour growth, invasion, metastasis and drug resistance. They do this by controlling chromatin, transcription, microRNA activity and protein function. Because lncRNA expression is often specific to tissues and disease states, and lncRNAs can be detected in blood and extracellular vesicles, they are attractive candidates for diagnosis, prognosis and treatment. However, the literature is large and uneven. Many findings come from small, single-cohort studies, and few have been independently validated. A critical review is needed to separate well-supported lncRNAs from preliminary reports and to identify which are closest to clinical use.


This review, now in preparation, will bring together current evidence on lncRNAs in NSCLC in four parts. First, it will outline how lncRNAs act in lung cancer cells, including as competing endogenous RNAs that sponge microRNAs. Second, it will summarise their roles in tumour growth, metastasis and the main NSCLC subtypes. Third, it will examine their contribution to resistance to chemotherapy, EGFR-targeted drugs and immunotherapy. Fourth, it will assess their potential as tissue and circulating biomarkers and as therapeutic targets. Throughout, the review will weigh the strength of the evidence, considering cohort size, independent validation and experimental support. It will also highlight open questions and the steps needed to move promising lncRNAs towards the clinic.
